Planar Standard is a constructed Magic: The Gathering format built around a
smaller card pool than Standard, rotating on a faster schedule. It is run by the
people who play it: the rules, the legal pool, and the event calendar are all
community decisions.

## Why a smaller pool

A narrower pool makes a deck cheaper to build and easier to change. It also means
a new set actually moves the metagame instead of adding a handful of cards to a
settled one, which is most of the fun of a rotating format.

Six sets are legal at a time, with **Foundations** as a permanent core. See
[Rules & legality](/rules) for the current list and the construction rules.

## What this site is for

Every format eventually generates the same questions — what is people playing,
what beats what, who is winning — and answers them from memory. This site answers
them from the event data instead.

- **Metagame analytics** from the decklists submitted at real events.
- **An Elo leaderboard** replayed from the match ledger, never hand-adjusted.
- **Decklist validation** against the legal pool, so a list is checked before
  round one rather than during it.
- **Articles**, both [format news](/news) and [community writing](/articles).

Everything published here is derived from data that is in the open, by code that
is in the open. Where a number could be misread, the page says so: every rate is
shown with the number of observations behind it, and a rate with too few is
withheld rather than shown small. [How the numbers are made](/methodology) is a
page rather than a footnote.

## Who runs it

The format is organised through the community's Discord and posts on
[r/planarMTG](https://www.reddit.com/r/planarMTG/). Organisers run the events;
anyone can [submit results and lists](/organizers), and anyone can
[write for the site](/getting-started).

Planar Standard exists to create fun, engaging games of Magic. Everyone is
welcome, preferences differ, and the focus stays on playing good games and
keeping the community a healthy one.
