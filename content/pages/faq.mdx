## The format

### Is the six-set pool a rolling window or a fixed list?

A fixed list. It is maintained as data, so it changes when the community changes
it rather than automatically on a release date. Special events may define their
own pool without changing the format.

### Are the deckbuilding rules the same as Standard?

Yes: 60-card maindeck minimum, 15-card sideboard maximum, four copies of any card
by name, basic lands exempt.

### Can I play an un-set card, or a Secret Lair printing?

Yes. Legality is a property of the **oracle card**, and every printing of a legal
card is playable. If the card is in the pool, your copy is fine whatever set it
came from.

### Does Foundations ever rotate?

No. It is the format's permanent core set. The other five rotate.

### What is banned?

Bans are announced by the community and published on [Rules & legality](/rules).
They are stored as data, which is why a B&R announcement takes effect without
anyone deploying a website.

## Playing

### Where do events happen?

On melee mostly, and on Challonge. Both are announced in the community Discord.

### Do I need a full playset of anything?

Rarely, and never a specific printing. The pool is small and the format is
deliberately cheap to enter.

### My decklist has a card the validator could not find. Did it reject the list?

No. An unresolved card name keeps the list and flags that one line. The deck is
still recorded; it is only held out of the card-level statistics until the name
is fixed.

## The numbers on this site

### Why does a win rate sometimes say "insufficient data" instead of a number?

Because the number would be a lie. A deck that goes 3-0 has not demonstrated a
100% win rate. Rates below a sample-size floor are withheld rather than shown
small, and every rate that is shown carries the number of observations behind it
and a confidence interval. [Methodology](/methodology) has the thresholds.

### What does "win rate of decks including this card" mean?

Exactly that, and not "the card's win rate". A card does not win a game; the deck
around it does. Strong cards appear in strong decks, so the number describes the
decks, not the card.

### Why is an event listed but not affecting the leaderboard?

Because it reported standings but not pairings. Ratings need to know who played
whom, and pairings are never guessed from final placements — guessing would
corrupt every rating downstream. The event still counts for metagame purposes.
See [How ratings work](/ratings-explained).

### Can a rating be corrected by hand?

No, and that is deliberate. The whole ladder is replayed from the match record
whenever anything changes. A rating is a function of the match history and the
rating settings, and of nothing else.

## Contributing

### Can I write an article?

Yes. Ask in the Discord. Tournament reports, deck primers, and arguments about
cards are all welcome, and you do not have to be an established name.

### I found a mistake in the data.

Please report it in the Discord. Corrections are recorded with a reason and
trigger a full recompute, so a fixed result propagates everywhere it should.

### Where is the code?

On [GitHub](https://github.com/planarstandard). Every statistic on this site is
derived, and the code that derives it is readable.
